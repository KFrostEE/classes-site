The subject in one picture

Semiconductor devices work by controlling small quantities of charge in small volumes. Radiation deposits energy in those same volumes, and that energy either liberates charge that the device did not intend to have, or displaces atoms from the lattice that carries the charge. Either way a device parameter moves, and once it moves far enough the circuit built from it fails.

The whole course is one causal chain:

The chain everything hangs on

A particle deposits energy in matter → that energy generates mobile charge or displaces atoms → the charge or damage shifts a device parameter → the parameter shift breaks a circuit.

The three named effects are three outcomes of that chain. Total ionizing dose is the cumulative result of liberated charge becoming trapped in insulating layers. Single-event effects are what happens when one particle deposits enough charge in one place at one instant. Displacement damage is the cumulative result of atoms being knocked out of the lattice. They share a mechanism and differ in which channel dominates and whether the result accumulates.

The course moves along that chain in order. Part I establishes what radiation is and how it couples to matter, then where it comes from and in what quantity. Part II quantifies the energy deposition. Parts III to V take each of the three effects in turn, from mechanism through to device behaviour. Part III ends with hardening by process, because it follows directly from the oxide physics. Part VI covers hardening by design and proving that a part will survive. The hardness-assurance module depends only on Part III and can be read straight after it.

Modules

Part I · Radiation and the environment

M01 · ~40 min
Radiation and Its Interaction with Matter

The particle and photon species that reach electronics, and the mechanism by which each transfers energy to a solid.
M02 · ~45 min
The Space Radiation Environment

Galactic cosmic rays, solar particles and the trapped belts — what each population is, how magnetic trapping works, and how orbit determines exposure.
M03 · ~40 min
Dose, Shielding and Terrestrial Environments

Dose and its units, exposure by orbit, why shielding saturates, and the atmospheric cascade that dominates at aircraft and ground level.

Part II · Energy deposition

M04 · ~55 min
Quantifying Energy Deposition

Stopping power and LET, the Bragg peak and range, electron–hole pair generation, dose enhancement, non-ionizing energy loss, radiation-induced interference, and how a dose rate is measured with a photodiode and transferred between materials.
M05 · ~30 min
The Three Effects: A Taxonomy

Ionizing against non-ionizing, crossed with cumulative against single-particle. Where TID, SEE and DD each sit, and why.

Part III · Total ionizing dose

M06 · ~40 min
Device Physics for Radiation Effects

MOS electrostatics and C–V, the charge centroid, threshold voltage and subthreshold slope, interface-trap occupancy, bipolar base current and gain, isolation structures, and charge-coupled devices.
M07 · ~50 min
Oxide-Trapped and Interface-Trap Charge

Charge yield against oxide field, polaron hopping transport, the net-oxide-trap-charge equation, E′ and Pb centres, latent interface-trap buildup, and the midgap technique that separates the two.
M08 · ~70 min
TID Effects on Devices and ICs

Threshold shift in service and rebound, stretchout and mobility loss, why isolation oxides fail before gate oxides, layout hardening and enclosed layout transistors, SOI back-channel leakage, scaling, bipolar gain and leakage, enhanced low-dose-rate sensitivity, imagers, and why circuit response is nonlinear in device degradation.
M09 · ~45 min
Oxide Charge in Time, Temperature, Field and Bias

How fast and how much: hole transport against temperature, field and thickness, worst-case and switched bias, trap depths by thermally stimulated current, tunnelling and emission fronts, electron traps, thin oxides, interface-trap kinetics, the >125 °C interface-trap anneal and the 100 °C bake, and cryogenic behaviour.
M10 · ~50 min
Gate Oxide Growth, Processing and Hardening by Process

How the gate oxide is grown and annealed, and why that fixes its radiation response: SiO₂ constants, the Deal–Grove law, high-κ stacks, oxide, interface and border traps, high-field stress as the twin of irradiation, net against total charge, and the process levers of hardening.

Part IV · Single-event effects

M11 · ~55 min
Single-Event Effects

What one particle does to a circuit: a strike resolved in time, funnelling at a reverse-biased junction, critical charge and the memory-cell upset, transients, the cross-section and its Weibull fit, effective LET, orbital error rates, scaling, and latchup, gate rupture and burnout.

Part V · Displacement damage

M12 · ~45 min
Displacement Damage and NIEL

Recoil spectra from collision kinematics, point defects against clusters and subcascades, lifetime and Messenger–Spratt gain, where NIEL scaling fails, annealing, imagers and solar cells, and single-particle damage in nanoscale devices.

Part VI · Hardening and assurance

M13 · pending
Rad-Hard Circuit Design: TID

Process against design hardening, enclosed layout transistors, guard rings, and what a commercial foundry will allow.
M14 · pending
Rad-Hard Circuit Design: SEE

Redundancy, triple modular redundancy, error correction, DICE cells, triple well — and what each costs in area and power.
M15 · ~55 min
Total-Dose Hardness Assurance

Why every lot is tested, why no single irradiation predicts a MOS part while a fast exposure and a 100 °C bake bound it (MIL-STD-883 Test Method 1019), why enhanced low-dose-rate sensitivity breaks that method for linear bipolar circuits, and how hydrogen, burn-in and aging move the answer. Depends only on Part III.

Conversions used in the course: 1 Gy = 100 rad · 1 Sv = 100 rem, so 10 mSv = 1 rem · H = wRD, so rem and rad coincide only where wR = 1, that is for photons and electrons.

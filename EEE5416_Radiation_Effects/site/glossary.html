Particles and photons

TermWhat it isKey factsWhy it matters
Photon
X-ray, gamma ray		 A quantum of electromagnetic radiation. No charge, no rest mass. X-ray and gamma differ by origin, not wavelength: gamma from nuclear transitions, X-ray from electron transitions or machine bremsstrahlung. Test sources: Co-60 at 1.17 and 1.33 MeV, Cs-137 at 662 keV. The standard total-dose test species. Ionizes indirectly, via the electron it liberates.
Electron
beta particle, β		 Elementary particle, charge −1e. Called a beta particle when emitted from a nucleus. Rest mass 0.511 MeV/c². Outer Van Allen belt: 0.1–10 MeV, max ~7 MeV. Dominant trapped species at GEO. Stopped readily by aluminium, but generates bremsstrahlung doing so.
Proton Hydrogen nucleus, charge +1e. Rest mass 938 MeV/c². Inner belt >100 MeV, some >400 MeV. ~89% of GCR, 90–95% of solar particles. The most abundant energetic species in space. Causes upsets indirectly via nuclear reactions, and directly below ~65 nm.
Neutron Uncharged nucleon. Rest mass 940 MeV/c². Free neutron mean lifetime ≈ 15 min, decaying to proton + electron + antineutrino. Dominates the atmospheric environment. No Coulomb interaction, so it cannot be stopped — only attenuated.
Alpha particle
α		 Helium-4 nucleus: two protons, two neutrons, charge +2e. Rest mass 3727 MeV/c². Range: a few cm of air, or a sheet of paper. ~9% of GCR. In microelectronics the source is usually the package itself — trace uranium and thorium in mould compound and solder.
Heavy ion Any nucleus heavier than helium, stripped of electrons. Charge +Ze. 1–2% of GCR. Defines the worst-case LET a part must survive. Ionizes densely enough to upset or destroy a node with no intermediary. The species accelerator testing is spent on.
Delta ray A secondary electron liberated with enough kinetic energy to ionize further atoms itself. Spreads ionization laterally away from the primary track. Widens the volume from which a circuit node can collect charge.

Interaction mechanisms

TermWhat it isKey factsWhy it matters
Ionization Transfer of enough energy to free a bound electron, leaving an ion pair. Threshold ~10 eV. Pair creation costs 3.6 eV in Si, 17 eV in SiO₂. The only process that produces mobile charge. Everything else matters through how much ionization it causes.
Excitation Promotion of a bound electron to a higher state without freeing it. Occurs at larger distances from the track than ionization. Atom de-excites, emitting a low-energy UV photon. Removes energy from the incident particle without producing free charge.
Bremsstrahlung Radiation emitted when a charged particle is deflected by a nuclear Coulomb field. Scales as Z² of the absorber and E/m² of the particle. Radiative fraction ≈ ZE/800, E in MeV. Sets the floor in a dose-depth curve. A high-Z shield converts stopped electrons into penetrating X-rays.
Cherenkov radiation Light emitted when a charged particle exceeds the phase velocity of light in a dielectric. Minor energy-loss channel; source of the blue glow around a reactor core. Background in radiation detectors.
Photoelectric absorption A photon is absorbed entirely by a bound electron, which is ejected. Cross-section rises steeply as photon energy falls and atomic number rises. Dominant in Si at 10 keV. Its strong Z dependence is what produces dose enhancement.
Compton scattering A photon transfers part of its energy to an electron and continues, degraded. Dominant at intermediate energy. Co-60 gammas interact with Si this way. The mechanism behind standard Co-60 total-dose testing.
Pair production A photon converts into an electron–positron pair in a nuclear field. Threshold 1.022 MeV — twice the electron rest energy. Third photon channel; only relevant above ~1 MeV.
Dose enhancement Local dose increase near a high-Z / low-Z interface, from the imbalance of secondary electrons crossing it. Largest for low-energy photons and high atomic number, and a significant issue for 10 keV X-ray testing. The largest measured factors, 8–10× in thin oxides, are beside very high-Z layers such as gold near 100 keV; no more than 2–3× in typical well photocurrent. A part can absorb far more dose than the incident fluence suggests, purely from its packaging and metallisation. Distinct from ELDRS.
Neutron capture A nucleus absorbs a neutron and emits a charged product — (n,α), (n,p). 10B(n,α)7Li releases ~2.8 MeV. Strongly favoured at thermal energies. Boron in dielectrics made this a leading terrestrial soft-error mechanism. Why thermal-neutron testing is a separate step.
Charged-particle equilibrium The condition in which as many secondary electrons enter a small volume as leave it. Holds inside a uniform material under photon irradiation; fails within about one secondary-electron range of an interface between materials of different atomic number. Dose from a photon field is computable from tabulated coefficients only where it holds. Where it fails, the correction is the dose enhancement factor.
Displacement
non-ionizing energy loss		 Energy transferred to a nucleus, knocking the atom off its lattice site. Produces a vacancy and an interstitial. Does not anneal at room temperature. Introduces bandgap states; degrades minority-carrier lifetime. The origin of displacement damage.
Primary knock-on atom
PKA		 The first atom displaced by an incident particle, which then displaces others as it travels. A 50 keV PKA is typical of a 1 MeV neutron or an energetic proton — enough to produce a cascade rather than one isolated defect. The recoil spectrum, not the incident particle alone, determines the defect structure left behind.
Frenkel pair A vacancy and the interstitial atom that left it. Roughly 90% recombine promptly; about 10% survive as stable defects. Only the surviving fraction degrades the device, which is why displacement damage is far smaller than the raw count of displacements suggests.

Quantities and units

TermWhat it isUnitsWatch out for
Stopping power
S = −dE/dx		Energy a charged particle loses per unit path length. Splits into electronic stopping, which ionizes, and nuclear stopping, which displaces atoms.MeV/cmScales as z²/β² by the Bethe expression, so it rises as the particle slows. Electronic stopping dominates for every particle in this course.
LET
linear energy transfer		Energy deposited per unit path length, divided by density. Used interchangeably with stopping power in this course.MeV·cm²/mg here; MeV·cm²/g in stopping-power tables; keV/µm in the radiological literature (1 MeV·cm²/mg = 233 keV/µm in Si)Normalised by density, hence the mass in the denominator. Formally the local deposition, so it differs from stopping power by the energy delta rays carry away. Anchors in Si: minimum ionizing 1.66×10⁻³, 100 MeV proton 5.9×10⁻³, accelerator ions 6–35.
Bragg peakThe maximum of energy deposition per unit length, close to the end of a charged particle’s track.—Charge collection is greatest when the peak coincides with the sensitive volume, which is what a heavy-ion test must arrange. Past the peak the ion captures electrons and deposition falls to zero.
Range
R = ∫dE/S		Total path a charged particle travels before stopping.g/cm², or a length after dividing by density. 5.5 MeV alpha in Si: CSDA 6.49 mg/cm² = 27.9 µmFrom NIST PSTAR/ASTAR or SRIM, never by hand. Straggling spreads the endpoint by of order 1%. The straight-path assumption fails for electrons, which need an extrapolated range instead.
Pair creation energy
Ep		Mean energy deposited per electron–hole pair produced.3.6 eV in Si · 17 eV in SiO₂Exceeds the bandgap because energy also goes to phonons and to carrier kinetic energy. Near Ep ≈ 2.8Eg + (0–1 eV) across semiconductors. A MOS device contains both materials and the constants are not interchangeable.
NIEL
non-ionizing energy loss		The part of the stopping power that goes into displacing atoms rather than ionizing. The exact counterpart of LET for the other channel.MeV·cm²/gCounts only recoils above the displacement threshold. Tabulated per particle, energy and target; used from tables.
DDD
displacement damage dose, Φ·NIEL		Non-ionizing energy deposited per unit mass — the non-ionizing counterpart of total dose.MeV/gDegradation scales with the product, which is what transfers a damage factor between particles. Checked per material: GaAs departs from NIEL scaling above roughly 40 MeV protons.
Displacement threshold
Td		Minimum energy a recoiling atom needs to leave its lattice site permanently.≈21 eV in SiBelow it the energy becomes lattice vibration and no defect remains. Roughly six times the 3.6 eV that liberates a carrier.
DEF
dose enhancement factor		Dose actually deposited in the sensitive region divided by the dose under charged-particle equilibrium.dimensionlessUp to 8–10 in thin oxides beside gold near 100 keV; no more than 2–3 in typical well photocurrent — the difference is layer thickness against the secondary-electron range. Atomic-number contrast peaks near 50–150 keV for gold against silicon.
Mass energy-absorption coefficient
µen/ρ		Converts a photon energy fluence into an absorbed dose in a named material.cm²/g, tabulated by NISTThe route to dose for photons, which have no LET. Strongly material- and energy-dependent, which is why dose enhancement exists.
Absorbed dose
D		Mean energy imparted per unit mass at a point: energy entering the volume minus energy leaving, divided by the mass.rad, or gray. 1 rad = 100 erg/g = 0.01 J/kg = 6.24×10¹³ eV/g; 1 Gy = 1 J/kg = 100 rad = 6.24×10¹⁵ eV/gAlways name the material — rad(Si) and rad(SiO₂) are not interchangeable, and a dose with no stated shield depth cannot be compared with another.
Dose rateDose per unit time.rad(Si)/s. Space: 10⁻⁶ to mid-10⁻³. 1 rad/s = 3.156×10⁷ rad/yrA separate requirement from total dose, not a restatement of it: oxides anneal during a slow irradiation, and bipolar devices degrade more at low rate.
FluenceParticles per unit area, integrated over time.cm⁻²Distinct from flux, which is per unit time.
FluxParticles per unit area per unit time.cm⁻²s⁻¹—
Cross-section
σ, single-event		Errors observed per unit fluence.cm²/deviceThree quantities here carry cm²: cross-section (cm²/device), LET (MeV·cm²/mg) and dose. Do not blur them.
FITFailures in time.1 per 10⁹ device-hours—
g0Initial electron–hole pair density generated per unit dose in SiO₂.8.1×10¹² pairs/cm³ per rad(SiO₂)Material-specific. Reproducible from 100 erg/g·rad ÷ 17 eV × density.
Equivalent dose
H = wRD		Absorbed dose in tissue, weighted by particle type for biological effect.rem, or sievert. 1 Sv = 100 rem, so 10 mSv = 1 remA tissue quantity — never appears in a device specification. Rem and rad coincide only where wR = 1. The course's "1 rem ≈ 1 rad to within 15–20%" describes the mixed atmospheric aircrew field specifically, not particles in general.
Radiation weighting factor
wR		Multiplier converting absorbed dose to equivalent dose, reflecting how densely a species deposits energy along its track.dimensionlessICRP 103: 1 photons, electrons, muons; 2 protons and charged pions; 20 alphas, heavy ions, fission fragments; for neutrons a continuous function of energy from 2.5 to 20.7 at 1 MeV. ICRP 60 gave 5 for protons and a 5–20 step for neutrons, so older references differ.
Areal density
mass thickness, xρ = ρt		Mass of material per unit area along the beam path.g/cm². Aluminium: 1 mm = 0.270 g/cm², 200 mils = 1.37 g/cm²The correct variable for shield depth and for every range table, because energy loss follows the number of electrons encountered rather than the length. Equates materials only to first order.

The space and terrestrial environment

TermWhat it isKey factsWhy it matters
Galactic cosmic rays
GCR		Nearly isotropic flux of energetic nuclei from outside the solar system.~89% protons, ~9% helium, 1–2% heavier ions and electrons. Flux peaks 100–1000 MeV/nucleon; max ~10²⁰ eV.Essentially unshieldable. Sets the heavy-ion single-event requirement; contributes little else in Earth orbit.
Solar particle event
SPE, flare, CME		Episodic burst of energetic particles from the Sun.90–95% protons. Flux can rise ~10⁴× in a large flare. CMEs can exceed 5×10⁵ protons/cm²·s.Episodic, tied to the ~11-year solar intensity peak. Drives worst-case mission planning.
Van Allen belts
trapped radiation		Particles confined by the geomagnetic field, gyrating, bouncing and drifting.Protons: one continuous belt from L = 1.14 outward, no slot; several hundred MeV, peak >10 MeV flux ~10⁵ cm⁻²s⁻¹ at L ≈ 1.7 (course extent ~1,000–12,000 km), stable over years. Electrons: inner zone L = 1–2, slot L = 2–3, outer zone L = 3–10 to ~7–10 MeV, ~3×10⁶ cm⁻²s⁻¹ above 1 MeV at L = 4–4.5, variable by orders of magnitude. 1 RE = 6,380 km.The dominant total-dose source for most Earth orbits. Fixed geography, so it can be designed around.
Solar windSteady outflow of plasma from the Sun, carrying an embedded magnetic field.Protons, alphas and electrons at eV to keV energies — too low to penetrate a spacecraft wall.Not a bulk damage source. It modulates GCR, shapes the magnetosphere, and charges exposed surfaces.
Solar flareSudden localized brightening of the Sun: a burst of electromagnetic radiation released by coronal magnetic reconnection.Electron-rich, lasts hours, high ³He/⁴He. Often but not always accompanied by energetic particles.Distinct from a CME, which is what produces the large proton fluences.
Coronal mass ejection
CME		Eruption of magnetized plasma that drives a shock and accelerates particles.Proton-rich, lasts days, low ³He. 10¹⁵–10¹⁷ g at 50–2,500 km/s, average ~450 km/s; hours to days to reach Earth.The source of the large solar particle events and of the geomagnetic storms that reshape the belts.
Solar activity cyclePeriodic variation in solar output.~11 years in intensity, ~22 years in magnetic polarity. Tracked by sunspot number and the 10.7 cm radio flux F10.7.GCR peak at solar minimum and solar events at solar maximum, so no single phase is worst for everything.
HeliosphereMagnetised cavity blown in the interstellar medium by the solar wind, bounded by the heliopause.Scatters incoming GCR; the lower the energy, the less likely a particle is to reach 1 AU. Modulation is negligible above ~20 GeV/nucleon.The reason GCR flux is anti-correlated with solar activity.
CRAND
cosmic ray albedo neutron decay		The source process for the inner proton belt.GCR make atmospheric neutrons; some escape upward and decay (mean life ≈ 880 s) into protons that the field traps.Ties the inner belt to the GCR flux, so it too is stronger at solar minimum.
Slot regionLocal minimum in electron flux between the inner and outer electron zones. An electron feature only — the proton belt runs straight through these shells.L ≈ 2–3. Storms inject electrons that then decay with a ~10 day time constant.Attractive for some missions, but highly variable.
South Atlantic Anomaly
SAA		Region where the offset geomagnetic field lets the inner proton belt reach unusually low altitude.A LEO spacecraft takes most of its trapped-proton dose and upsets here.Makes LEO exposure episodic and predictable — schedulable.
LEO / MEO / GEOOrbit bands.LEO ≤ 2,000 km. MEO includes GPS at 20,200 km. GEO at 35,800 km.Orbit choice changes annual dose by ~1000×.
Atmospheric cascadeShower of secondaries produced when a cosmic ray strikes the upper atmosphere. A primary interacts with N or O, producing pions, neutrons, protons and fragments; π± decay to muons, π⁰ to photon pairs that start electromagnetic showers.Sea level presents 1,033 g/cm², 12 km presents 197 g/cm². Neutrons dominate at aircraft altitude, where the upset rate is ~300× the sea-level value.TID is not a concern in avionics; single-event effects are, because a neutron needs no accumulated dose to flip a bit.
Pfotzer–Regener maximumAltitude at which the atmospheric cascade peaks — the balance point between build-up of secondaries and their absorption.15–20 km, which is 56–123 g/cm² of atmospheric depth. Aircraft cruise below it, at 10–13 km.Neutron flux is not monotonic in altitude. Above the maximum the neutron flux falls while primary ion flux keeps rising, so the upset mechanism shifts from indirect to direct ionization.
Terrestrial neutron environmentThe ground-level remnant of the cascade.JEDEC reference: New York City, sea level, 3.6×10⁻³ neutrons cm⁻²s⁻¹ above 10 MeV ≈ 13 cm⁻²h⁻¹. A 90 nm SRAM runs 777 FIT/Mbit.A small flux acting on a very large number of nodes: one error per Tbit per 1.3 hours. The reason ECC is standard in server, automotive and avionics memory.
Man-made environmentsRadiation fields produced deliberately, as opposed to encountered.Accelerators (LHC detectors, projected into the Grad range), reactors and their accident conditions, and nuclear weapon environments. The Van Allen belts are not among them.Some systems must survive them; all of them double as the ground test facilities used to qualify parts.
Weapon environmentThe field from a nuclear detonation.Prompt gamma rays, X-rays hot and cold, neutrons, and the secondary charged particles those produce. Delivered as a pulse.Dose-rate effects and prompt photocurrents dominate rather than slow trapping, and the X-ray component drives dose enhancement at high-Z interfaces.

Shielding

TermWhat it isKey factsWhy it matters
Dose-depth curveDose behind a given depth of shielding, plotted against that depth.Decomposes into trapped electrons, which fall steeply and stop; bremsstrahlung, made inside the shield, which attenuates slowly; and trapped protons, which fall slowly because the spectrum reaches hundreds of MeV.The total flattens once the electrons are gone. That flattening is why shielding saturates.
Extrapolated range
Katz–Penfold		Depth of material beyond which essentially no electrons of a given energy emerge.In aluminium, from a Katz–Penfold fit to aluminium absorbers: 1 MeV → 0.41 g/cm² (1.5 mm); 3 MeV → 1.48 g/cm² (5.5 mm); 7 MeV → 3.60 g/cm² (13.4 mm); 10 MeV → 5.19 g/cm² (19.2 mm). A 100 MeV proton needs 9.99 g/cm², or 37 mm.Sets the depth at which the electron contribution disappears. Not the same as the CSDA range, which is longer because it follows the wandering path.
Shielding saturationThe point beyond which additional shield mass removes very little further dose.Reached once the shield exceeds the electron range. What remains is bremsstrahlung generated in the shield and the high-energy end of the proton spectrum.Makes shielding a bounded solution. Beyond it the remaining options are part selection, spot shielding and orbit choice.
Why aluminiumThe standard spacecraft shield and structure material.Mass is the binding constraint; the radiative-to-collisional stopping ratio ≈ ZE/700 is 1.9% in Al and 11.7% in Pb at 1 MeV, higher in proportion to atomic number, so lead diverts far more energy into penetrating photons at equal areal density; and a conductive bonded skin prevents differential charging.High Z is right only for spot shielding one small part, or as the front layer of a graded stack backed by a low-Z absorber.
Spacecraft chargingAccumulation of differing electrostatic potentials on a vehicle with no ground.Sunlit surfaces photoemit while shadowed surfaces collect plasma electrons; differential potentials can reach kilovolts and discharge through the structure.An established cause of on-orbit anomalies, and the reason the shield must also be conductive and bonded.

Magnetospheric geometry and trapping

TermWhat it isKey factsWhy it matters
MagnetosphereThe region in which the Earth's magnetic field controls charged-particle motion.Internal field approximately dipolar to L = 4–5; axis tilted ~11.5°, centre offset >500 km. Compressed to ~10 RE sunward (~6 in a storm), tail to ~1,000 RE.It creates the belts and filters everything else.
L-shell
(B, L) coordinates		Label for a drift shell: the geocentric distance, in Earth radii, at which its field line crosses the magnetic equator.L = 1 + heq/RE at the equator, with RE = 6,380 km. B gives position along the line.Trapped flux is a function of (B, L) alone, not of geography. Measured from the Earth's centre, not its surface.
Gyroradius
rg		Radius of the circular component of a charged particle's helical path.rg = p⊥/(|q|B); 235 km for a 100 MeV proton at L = 1.7.Small against the shell radius, which is why the guiding-centre picture works.
Pitch angle
α		Angle between a particle's velocity and the local field.sin²α/B is constant, so α grows as the field converges.Decides where a particle mirrors, and so whether it stays trapped.
Mirror pointWhere the velocity along the field reaches zero and the particle reverses.Bm = Beq/sin²αeq. Bounce period a few tenths of a second.The confinement mechanism along a field line.
Loss coneThe small equatorial pitch angles whose mirror point would lie inside the atmosphere.Sets the belts' lower edge near 200 km altitude.Any process that scatters pitch angle toward the field direction empties the belt.
Drift shellToroidal surface swept out by a trapped particle's azimuthal drift.Protons drift west and electrons east, with a period of tens of seconds to tens of minutes that falls with energy. The ring current is this same motion in the 10–200 keV ions near L = 3–8.The surface labelled by L, around which the environment is averaged.
Magnetic rigidity
R		Momentum per unit charge — a particle's resistance to magnetic deflection.R = pc/Ze, in volts and usually GV; 0.445 GV for a 100 MeV proton.Equal rigidity means an equal trajectory, whatever the species.
Geomagnetic cutoff
Störmer cutoff		Minimum rigidity needed to reach a point in the magnetosphere.Rc ≈ 14.9 cos⁴λ/(r/RE)² GV. Geomagnetic storms lower it.Why polar orbits admit solar protons and equatorial ones do not — inclination matters as much as altitude.

Critical charge
Qcrit		Minimum collected charge that flips a node.—Set by the technology and circuit, never by the incident ion.

Defects, traps and charge in oxides

TermWhat it isKey factsWhy it matters
Oxide-trapped charge
ΔNot, ΔVot		 Holes captured at deep traps in an insulator after ionizing radiation, mostly within a few nm of the Si/SiO₂ interface. Net positive. Maximum immediately after irradiation, then anneals. Shift scales as tox2. Shifts VT negative in n- and p-channel devices alike. M07 §3.
Interface traps
Dit (cm⁻²eV⁻¹), Nit (cm⁻²)		 Electrically active states at the Si/SiO₂ boundary whose charge depends on the surface potential. Donor-like below midgap, acceptor-like above, so net charge is zero at midgap. Minimum immediately after irradiation, then grows over ~10³ s. Shifts n-channel VT positive and p-channel negative; stretches the subthreshold curve; raises bipolar base current. M06 §5, M07 §8.
Charge yield
fy(Eox)		 Fraction of radiation-generated holes that escapes prompt recombination. Rises with oxide field, falls with the ionization density of the particle. At 1 MV/cm: ≈0.9 Co-60 and 12 MeV electrons, ≈0.55 5 keV electrons, ≈0.13 700 keV protons, <0.1 2 MeV alphas. A dose without its source does not predict a threshold shift. M07 §2.
Hole trapping efficiency
fot		 Fraction of the pairs generated that ends as trapped charge. Defined by fot = ΔNot/(toxkgfyfDED). Process-dependent. The quantity a total-dose measurement returns, and the last term in the course's master equation. M07 §3.
Pair generation constant
kg, also written g0		 Electron–hole pairs created per unit volume per unit dose. 8.1×10¹² pairs/(cm³·rad) in SiO₂, 4×10¹³ in Si, ~7×10¹³ in GaAs. Deck 05 writes kg, Deck 03 writes g0 — same quantity. Converts dose to charge. M04 §4, M07 §1.
Dose enhancement factor
fDE		 Ratio of the dose a layer actually absorbs to the dose stated, when a neighbouring layer of different atomic number floods it with secondary electrons. ≈1 for Co-60; >1 for low-energy X-rays beside high-Z layers. The professor's worked 10 keV X-ray value is 1.55. Why an X-ray result and a Co-60 result do not correlate without correction. M04 §5, M07 §§2–3.
E′ centre An unpaired electron highly localized on a silicon atom bonded to three oxygens. Formed when a hole breaks the weak Si–Si bond of an oxygen vacancy. At least nine variants. Paramagnetic, so visible to EPR. The defect that holds oxide-trapped charge. Correlation with ΔNot is strong in some oxides, weaker in others including SOI buried oxides. M07 §6.
Pb centre A silicon dangling bond at the Si/SiO₂ interface. Created when a proton released by a transporting hole depassivates a Si–H bond. Also identified by EPR. The defect that is an interface trap. M07 §8.
Border traps Near-interface oxide traps close enough to the silicon to exchange charge with it on the timescale of an I–V measurement. Electrostatically oxide traps, kinetically interface traps. Separated from true interface traps by charge pumping. The reason the two-population picture needs a measurement that can tell them apart. M07 §9.
Latent interface-trap buildup A second rise in Dit long after irradiation ends. Occurs around 10⁶ s, can increase interface traps up to fourfold, seen in oxides with high vacancy densities, initiated by H₂ transport, EA ≈ 0.41 eV. A part can degrade further eleven days after a test ends. A concern for space systems. M07 §8.
Polaron hopping
dispersive transport, CTRW		 A hole in amorphous SiO₂ localizes on an oxygen site and moves only by thermally assisted hops to a neighbour. Hole mobility 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻⁷ cm²/V·s against ~20 cm²/V·s for electrons. Recovery spans many decades in time and scales as tox⁴. Why oxides charge positive, and why an irradiated device keeps changing after the beam stops. M07 §5.
Fixed oxide charge
QF		 Positive charge within a few nm of the interface, created during oxidation. Bias-independent and immobile. Distinct from radiation-induced trapped charge. Translates the C–V curve. M06 §3.
Mobile ionic charge
QM		 Alkali contamination, principally sodium and potassium, that drifts under bias at elevated temperature. Signature is a flat-band voltage that depends on bias–temperature history — hysteresis in a swept measurement. A process-control problem that mimics radiation damage. M06 §3.
Electron traps Sites in thermal oxide that capture electrons rather than holes. In some gate oxides their number exceeds half that of hole traps. They compensate trapped holes without removing them. Measured ΔVot is a net quantity and understates the holes actually trapped. M07 §7.

Device structures and measurements

TermWhat it isKey factsWhy it matters
Flat-band voltage
VFB		 Gate voltage at which the semiconductor bands are flat to the interface and there is no field in the substrate. At flat band all image charge returns to the gate, so the shift is the voltage dropped across the oxide between the gate and the charge. The reference point radiation-induced shifts are measured against. M06 §§1, 3.
Charge centroid
x̄		 Charge-weighted mean distance of oxide charge from the gate. ΔVFB = −(Qox/Cox)(x̄/tox). Charge at the gate does nothing; charge at the interface does the most. Radiation-induced holes pile up near the interface, so the weighting is close to 1. M06 §3.
Subthreshold swing
S, mV/decade		 Gate voltage needed to change drain current by one decade below threshold. Ideal minimum S0 = (kT/q)ln10 = 59.5 mV/dec at 300 K. Cit = qDit with Dit per eV. The course form omits Cd. Its slope measures Dit directly; stretchout is the interface-trap signature. M06 §6.
Midgap technique Separating ΔVot from ΔVit using one subthreshold sweep. ΔVot = ΔVmg; ΔVit = ΔVth − ΔVmg. Errors ±3% on ΔVth, ±5–10% on the components. Works because interface traps are neutral at midgap whatever their density. M07 §9.
Charge pumping
ICP		 Pulsing the gate between inversion and accumulation and measuring the recombination current at the substrate. ICP = qAGfDitΔΨs. Gate area in cm². Measures low Dit accurately and separates interface traps from border traps. M07 §9.
EPR / ESR
electron paramagnetic (spin) resonance		 Resonant microwave absorption by an unpaired electron in a swept magnetic field. The g-tensor depends on the defect's local environment, so different defects resonate at different fields. Identifies a defect's atomic structure rather than merely counting states — how E′ and Pb were established. M07 §6.
Thermally stimulated current
TSC		 Heating an irradiated capacitor at a controlled rate and recording the release current. A trap of a given depth empties at a characteristic temperature. Maps the energy distribution of trapped holes, which sets the thermal-emission annealing rate. M07 §§6–7.
Gummel plot Collector and base current plotted logarithmically against base–emitter voltage. Separates base-current components by ideality factor: n = 1 for neutral-base recombination, n = 2 for depletion-region recombination. Radiation-induced current usually falls between 1 and 2. Shows radiation-induced excess base current at low bias while IC is unchanged. M06 §7.
Lateral PNP A bipolar geometry in which carriers travel laterally just below the surface. Typically very sensitive to total dose, because the transit runs adjacent to the oxide rather than crossing it once. The most radiation-soft bipolar geometry, and common in analogue parts. M06 §7.
Field oxide
isolation oxide, STI		 Thick oxide separating neighbouring devices. Did not thin as gate oxides scaled, so it now holds far more trapped charge. Countered by a channel-stop implant that raises the parasitic threshold. Trapped charge inverts the p-type surface beneath it, opening a leakage path. The primary total-dose failure mechanism in advanced CMOS. M06 §8.
Buried oxide
BOX, back channel		 The insulator under the silicon island of an SOI transistor. Back-channel leakage is nearly independent of gate voltage; edge leakage increases with it, so the combined curve can show a step. Charge trapped in it inverts the underside of the body, connecting source to drain where the gate has no control. M06 §8.
Charge transfer inefficiency
CTI = 1 − CTE		 Fraction of a charge packet left behind in one CCD transfer. A packet making n transfers retains CTEn. A background charge fills traps and improves CTI, at the cost of noise. Multiplied by the length of the readout chain, so a tiny per-transfer loss smears an image. Degraded mainly by displacement damage. M06 §9.
Stretchout Deformation of the subthreshold characteristic along the voltage axis, as opposed to a rigid translation of it. Comes from interface traps, whose occupancy changes as the surface potential sweeps, so they add a bias-dependent capacitance in parallel with the semiconductor. Measured as an increase in S. The one signature that separates ΔNit from ΔNot on a single curve. M08 §2.
Rebound
superrecovery		 An n-channel threshold that moved negative during irradiation turning around, crossing its pre-irradiation value, and continuing positive. ΔVot is maximum at end of irradiation and anneals; ΔVit is minimum then and grows. Opposite signs, opposite time behaviour. Worst case can arrive long after the exposure, so qualification adds 50% dose plus 168 h biased anneal at 100 °C. M08 §3.
Field oxide
FOX; LOCOS or STI		 The thick oxide isolating one transistor from the next. LOCOS is grown through a mask; STI is an etched trench, filled. 200–1000 nm, one to two orders thicker than a gate oxide, so the same trapped areal charge develops volts rather than millivolts. LOCOS risk is the graded bird's beak at high field; STI risk is poor-quality deposited oxide. The primary total-dose failure mechanism in advanced CMOS — commercial ICs can fail at 5 krad(SiO2). M08 §5.
Enclosed layout transistor
ELT; edgeless		 Transistor whose gate is drawn as a closed ring, one diffusion inside and one outside. Effective W = perimeter through the middle of the ring, so W/L is set by geometry and there is a minimum device size. The two diffusions are not symmetric. The gate never crosses a thin-to-thick oxide edge, so no parasitic path exists. No leakage increase to 2 Mrad(SiO2) in 0.25 µm. M08 §6.
Guard ring
channel stop		 Heavily doped implant of the substrate type placed under the field oxide, drawn as a ring around a device. Raises the surface doping, so trapped positive charge that would invert a lightly doped surface no longer can. Hardens the field-oxide leakage path, and separately lowers single-event latch-up susceptibility. M08 §6.
Back-channel leakage Source-to-drain conduction along the underside of an SOI silicon film, through an inversion layer formed by charge trapped in the buried oxide. Nearly independent of the top gate voltage, unlike edge leakage, which the gate does control. The two together give a stepped characteristic. The total-dose penalty SOI pays for its single-event advantage. Broken by the BUSFET. M08 §7.
BUSFET
body-under-source FET		 SOI transistor whose source is bottomed by body silicon rather than reaching down to the buried oxide. Interrupts the back-channel path at its source end, so it cannot connect source to drain whatever the buried oxide holds. Structural hardening against BOX leakage, without changing the oxide. M08 §7.
Equivalent oxide thickness
EOT		 Thickness of SiO2 that would give the same capacitance per unit area as the actual gate dielectric. tEOT = tkεox/εk. A capacitance equivalence only — the physical layer is thicker, generates more pairs per unit area, and traps by its own defect chemistry. Why total dose is still specified in rad(SiO2) even for high-κ stacks. M04 §8.
Quasi-static C–V Capacitance measured from the displacement current driven by a slow gate-voltage ramp, rather than by an AC bridge. The inversion layer is replenished by thermal generation, which is far too slow to follow a 100 kHz or 1 MHz excitation, so a conventional meter always reports the high-frequency branch. The difference between the quasi-static and high-frequency curves measures Dit across the gap. M06 §2.
p–i–n photodiode Diode with a thick, almost undoped region between heavily doped contacts, fully depleted under reverse bias. Every pair generated inside is swept out before it can recombine, so collected charge equals generated charge and the current is the energy-absorption rate divided by Ep/q. The standard X-ray dosimeter in this field. M04 §8.

Advanced Topics in Microelectronics

Deep-submicron fabrication: how a silicon wafer becomes several hundred million transistors, and what had to be reinvented each time those transistors were made smaller.

Modules 10 of 20 built Assessment Midterm 30% · Final 40% · Project 30%

The subject in one picture

The catalogue calls this course "advanced topics in microelectronics." The professor narrowed it in the first ten minutes of the first lecture and has not moved since: the subject is advanced micro-fabrication. Device physics appears only where it is needed to explain why a process step exists.

One question organises the whole of it. The gate length of a MOSFET has been reduced in every technology generation since 1970, because drain current scales as the inverse of that length and because shorter devices pack more densely. Nothing else about the transistor tolerated that reduction unchanged. Each time the gate length fell far enough, some other part of the structure or the process stopped working, and something had to be invented to replace it. The nine chapters of this course are nine of those inventions.

What "doing well" requires here

Most of the assessable content is process sequences and the reasoning behind each step — why a spacer exists, why the implant goes through a screen oxide, why the slurry is alkaline. A smaller share is quantitative: the threshold voltage calculation above all, plus oxide capacitance, Fermi potential, depletion width, and band gap against wavelength. The professor works these at the board and has said explicitly that the course has no homework, so the formula sheet carries every equation and every worked example he has demonstrated. The rest is materials judgement: which high-k, which refractory metal, which resist, and why that one.

Modules

Twenty modules in seven parts, following the professor's chapter order except where a chapter divides more cleanly by topic: the first half of Chapter 3 is split into the engineering of the gate oxide (M05), its atomic structure (M05a), and the defects that carry charge (M05b); Chapter 4 into the high-k gate dielectric (M07) and the high-k memory capacitor (M07b). Modules shown with a dashed border are not yet written.

Part I · The device and the problem

M01 · background · ~45 min

The MOSFET and How One Is Made

The MOS capacitor through accumulation, depletion and inversion; threshold; the drain characteristic; the p-channel device and the CMOS inverter. Then the three-mask fabrication sequence that produces one. Assumed by the course and not taught by it — skip if you have had a devices course.

 M02 · ~40 min

The Deep-Submicron MOSFET: A Structural Roadmap

The gate-length history and the LE/tox ≈ 45 rule. The four axes along which the transistor was rebuilt — gate stack, gate dielectric, channel doping, drain structure — plus epitaxial substrates, dual-doped poly, STI and SOI. Closes on the scaling-limits table.

 M03 · ~50 min

The Threshold Voltage Equation

Work function, the photoelectric effect and electron affinity; why φMS is not a constant for silicon. Then the interface-charge, depletion-charge and Fermi-potential terms, each with its own physics. Why the aluminium gate became polysilicon, and then dual-doped polysilicon.

Part II · The substrate

M04 · ~40 min

Silicon Wafer Preparation and the 300 mm Wafer

Sand to metallurgical-grade silicon to trichlorosilane to the Siemens rod; Czochralski pulling, grinding, flats and notches, slicing, lapping, etching, edge contouring and polishing. Wafer diameter history and the area economics that drove it.

Part III · The gate stack

M05 · ~30 min

Thin Gate Oxides: Roles and Requirements

The issues the chapter sets out to answer. The many roles of SiO₂ in a process and the thickness each needs. Why the gate oxide must keep thinning and what that costs, and the ten requirements a deep-submicron gate dielectric has to meet, including the breakdown field that caps the supply voltage.

 M05a · ~45 min

Bonds and Structure: Silicon, Silicon Dioxide and Their Interface

The chemistry the chapter assumes, drawn. Covalent bonds, valence and electronegativity; sp³ silicon and the diamond lattice; the SiO₄ tetrahedron, bridging and non-bridging oxygen, and why thermal oxide is amorphous. How the oxide grows into the silicon, and why 0.46 of its thickness is consumed silicon. The ideal interface and the real transition region.

 M05b · ~55 min

Defects and Charge at the Si/SiO₂ Interface

The four oxide charges, each as a particular atom in a particular bonding state. The dangling bond as a state in the gap and the Pb centre; stretched Si–Si and Si–O bonds; the oxygen vacancy and the E′ centre. Hydrogen passivation, Si–OH, and the hot-carrier chain that breaks Si–H. Oxide traps from process radiation, and mobile ions in the open network.

 M06 · ~55 min

Thin Gate Oxides: Conduction and Breakdown

Fowler–Nordheim against direct tunnelling and the thickness at which each takes over. What the current does to the film — electron and hole trapping, and the opposite feedback each gives. SILC, the two stages of breakdown, polysilicon depletion, and the six factors that set the thinnest usable oxide. Then RTO, ISSG, and the nitrided, oxynitride and fluorinated films that bought the last few ångströms.

 M07 · ~60 min

High-k Gate Dielectrics

Why the dielectric constant has to rise once SiO₂ tunnels, and where a large one comes from. Equivalent oxide thickness and dielectrics in series. What else a replacement must satisfy — band offsets, stability against silicon, staying amorphous — and why the interfacial SiO₂ layer is deliberate. Atomic-layer deposition, then Ta₂O₅, Al₂O₃, ZrO₂, HfO₂, hafnium silicate and Pr₂O₃ in the notes' order, and the metal gate.

 M07b · ~65 min

High-k Capacitor Dielectrics for DRAM and FeRAM

The one-transistor, one-capacitor cell and why its charge must stay near 30 fF. Trench and stacked cells, hemispherical grain, and the electrodes that replace polysilicon. Ta₂O₅ capacitors; perovskites and ferroelectricity; BST films, their deposition and integration; and the FeRAM cell.

Part IV · The deep-submicron transistor

M08 · ~40 min

Well Engineering and Retrograde Wells

Why the diffused twin well fails below 0.5 µm — lateral spread, aspect ratio, boron segregation into the field oxide, thermal budget. High-energy implanted retrograde wells, the chained-implant recipe, and what retrograde buys against latch-up.

 M09 · ~45 min

Channel Engineering

The punchthrough condition and the doping-against-mobility trade it forces. Threshold-adjust implantation through a screen oxide, and why the peak is placed where it is. Super-steep retrograde profiles, why indium and antimony replace boron and phosphorus, and halo and pocket implants.

 M10 · ~50 min

Source–Drain Engineering

Lateral diffusion and gate overlap capacitance, and the self-aligned gate that removes them. The five-step lightly-doped-drain process with its spacer, reactive ion etch and reflow. Source–drain extension at 0.18 µm. Silicide and salicide. The parasitic series resistance budget.

Part V · Patterning

M11 · ~40 min

Advanced Lithography I: Resists

Resolution and depth of focus as the two competing limits. From g-line and i-line to deep ultraviolet. Chemically amplified resists, the 193 nm polymer families, resist collapse, and the anti-reflective coatings that make thin-film interference tolerable.

 M12 · ~45 min

Advanced Lithography II: Sources and Tricks

KrF, ArF and F₂ excimer sources and the optics each demands. Phase-shift masking. Optical proximity correction and off-axis illumination. Immersion. Extreme ultraviolet and its molybdenum–silicon multilayer mirrors. Electron-beam lithography and SCALPEL.

Part VI · Planarization and isolation

M13 · ~45 min

Chemical Mechanical Polishing: The Process

Why multilevel interconnect forces global planarization. The four degrees of planarization and the planarization factor. Platen, carrier, pad and slurry; the chemical-then-abrasive removal mechanism. Removal rate, degree of planarization, and within-die and wafer-to-wafer non-uniformity.

 M14 · ~40 min

CMP: Slurries, Damascene and Endpoint

Oxide slurries against metal slurries and why their chemistries differ. Tungsten and copper damascene. Dishing and erosion. Endpoint detection by interferometry, motor torque and backside infrared. Post-CMP cleaning.

 M15 · ~45 min

Shallow Trench Isolation

What LOCOS costs: bird's beak, field-oxide thinning at narrow spacings, stress-induced defects. The full STI flow — nitride hard mask, trench etch, liner oxidation, high-density-plasma fill, densification, CMP. Trench corner rounding and the parasitic sidewall device it prevents.

Part VII · On the syllabus, not yet in the notes

M16 · blocked

Low-k Dielectrics and Copper Interconnect

RC delay in the wiring, the candidate materials and their porosity problem, and integration with damascene. No source material has been provided. The professor signalled in Lecture 1 that this may become a project topic rather than a lecture.

 M17 · thin

Silicon-on-Insulator Technology

Why bulk silicon leaks between devices: latch-up, transient radiation paths, source- and drain-to-substrate capacitance. SIMOX and bonded SOI; partially against fully depleted; the floating body. The notes give this ten lines on page 12 and nothing more.

What is missing

Recorded so the gaps are visible rather than papered over.

  • No assignments of any kind. No homework, no quizzes, no old exams. The professor confirmed in Lecture 1 that the course has none. The single most useful thing that could be added to the sources is the midterm once it is returned.
  • Chapter 7 of the class notes does not exist. Chapter 6 ends on page 105 and Chapter 8 begins on page 106. Chapter 6 is titled "Advanced Lithography I", so a second lithography chapter is the obvious candidate — worth asking about.
  • The notes stop mid-sentence on page 145, inside the discussion of trench corner rounding. More pages are presumably still to come.
  • No dates. The syllabus schedule is sixteen empty rows. The midterm is "sometime in October" and the final is in finals week; neither is fixed.
  • Project topics have not been distributed. They are 30% of the grade and are assigned individually.

Assumed background

The professor was explicit in Lecture 1 that this course does not teach semiconductor fundamentals: band gap, electrons and holes, drift and diffusion, mobility, and the pn junction are all taken as given, as is the operation of a MOSFET. Basic fabrication — oxidation, diffusion, lithography — is an advantage rather than a requirement; each gets about ten minutes here and three weeks in EEE 5356. M01 exists to close that gap and can be skipped by anyone who has taken a devices course.

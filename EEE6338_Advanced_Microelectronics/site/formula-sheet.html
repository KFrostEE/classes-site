The two conventions that cause most wrong answers

Everything is in centimetres. This course works in CGS-derived units throughout, not SI. The permittivity of free space is ε0 = 8.85×10⁻¹⁴ F/cm, not 8.85×10⁻¹² F/m. Capacitance is per unit area in F/cm². Doping is in cm⁻³, areal charge density in cm⁻², charge per unit area in C/cm². Depletion widths come out in cm and are converted to µm afterwards. Convert every length to centimetres before substituting: 1 Å = 10⁻⁸ cm, 1 nm = 10⁻⁷ cm, 1 µm = 10⁻⁴ cm.

Densities are not charges. Interface and oxide charge are quoted in the literature and in lecture as areal number densities in cm⁻², and every equation here needs a charge in C/cm². Multiply by q = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C first. Omitting that step produces an answer wrong by nineteen orders of magnitude while still having the units of a voltage.

Which tunnelling mechanism is operating, or the gate leakage of a thin oxide Compare tox against 4 nm: above it and at high field, Fowler–Nordheim; at or below it, direct. Confirm F–N by plotting log(J/Eox2) against 1/Eox and checking for a straight line. (6.1)
The thinnest usable gate oxide, or whether a given oxide leaks too much Convert the off-state leakage spec to a current density over the channel area — the standard case gives 1 A/cm² — and compare. Cross-check against the reliability thicknesses: 110 Å at 5 V, 65 Å at 3.3 V, 45 Å at 2.5 V, each already carrying a 30% defect margin. Tunnelling becomes excessive below 1.4 nm. (6.2)
Why the measured gate capacitance is below Cox even in strong inversion at low frequency Polysilicon depletion. Put Cpoly dep in series with Cox; the smaller dominates. Interface traps stretch a C–V curve along the voltage axis instead, and never push it above Cox — that is how the two are told apart. (6.3)
Why a stressed oxide's current drifts with time, and in which direction Falling current at constant voltage means electron trapping — cathode field reduced, negative feedback. Rising current means hole trapping by anode hole injection — cathode field raised, positive feedback. An abrupt jump is breakdown. (6.1)

2 · How solutions work in this subject

2.1 Signs in the threshold equation

Three of the four terms change sign with the device type. Getting this wrong is the most common error in the whole course, and the result still looks plausible.

Termn-channel (p body)p-channel (n body)Rule
φMS−− Negative for Al and for n⁺ poly on either body type
−Qi/Cox−− Qi is positive charge in both cases
−Qd/Cox+− Qd < 0 for p body (ionised acceptors), > 0 for n body (ionised donors)
+2φF+− φF > 0 for p type, < 0 for n type

Consequence to sanity-check against. The third and fourth terms always share a sign, so they add rather than cancelling; for an n-channel device they come to about +1 V together and partially offset the first two. A p-channel device has all four negative, so its threshold is large and negative — several times the magnitude of an n-channel device on the same wafer with the same oxide. If a p-channel calculation produces a small or positive threshold, a sign is wrong. This asymmetry is why early MOS circuits needed six-volt supplies.

2.2 Order-of-magnitude checks

  • Cox for 1000 Å is 3.45×10⁻⁸ F/cm². Scale inversely with thickness: 100 Å gives 3.45×10⁻⁷, 20 Å gives 1.73×10⁻⁶ F/cm².
  • φF is 0.288 V at 10¹⁵ cm⁻³ and rises by 0.0259 × ln(10) = 0.060 V per decade of doping. At 10¹⁶ it is 0.348 V, at 10¹⁷ it is 0.408 V.
  • Wm is 0.867 µm at 10¹⁵ cm⁻³. It falls roughly as the inverse square root of the doping, so a decade of doping shrinks it by about 3.2×, less the slow growth of φF.
  • An interface charge density of 5×10¹¹ cm⁻² through a 1000 Å oxide costs 2.32 V. Scale linearly in both density and thickness.
  • A field of 10 MV/cm across 5 nm is 5 V; across 2 nm it is 2 V. Thickness in nm times 10 gives the breakdown voltage in volts at 10 MV/cm.

2.3 When a value looks wrong

Several numbers in the class notes and lectures disagree with the literature. The module pages flag each one where it appears, and the course follows the professor's version for anything assessed. The ones that affect a calculation are: ni = 1.5×10¹⁰ cm⁻³ (accepted value 9.65×10⁹, moves 2φF by 23 mV), and the p-channel worked answer of −4.919 V, which does not reconstruct from the stated terms — see M03 §7.1.

3 · The MOSFET

ID = ZL µnCox [(VG − VT)VD − ½VD2]
(5.1)
ID drain current (A) · Z channel width (cm) · L channel length (cm) · µn channel electron mobility (cm²/V·s) · Cox oxide capacitance per unit area (F/cm²) · voltages in V. Linear region only, valid while VD < VG − VT. The professor writes the channel width as Z; most texts write W. Z/L is typically 5–10. Taught in M05 §2.
ID,sat = ZµnCox2L (VG − VT)2
(1.2)
Same symbols as (5.1). Saturation region, valid once VD ≥ VG − VT, where the channel has pinched off at the drain end and further drain voltage falls across the pinch-off region rather than along the channel. Obtained by substituting VD = VG − VT into (5.1). Current is then independent of VD to first order and rises as the square of the gate overdrive. See M01 §4.
wfinal ≈ wmask + 2d   (isotropic wet etch)
(1.3)
wfinal opened width at the surface (µm) · wmask width of the window in the mask (µm) · d depth etched (µm). A wet chemical etch removes material at the same rate in every direction, so it eats sideways under the mask by about the depth it has gone down, on each of the two sides. A 2 µm window etched 1 µm deep opens to about 4 µm. A dry anisotropic etch — reactive ion etching — removes material almost only downward, so the opened width stays near 2 µm. This is the whole reason plasma etching replaced wet etching for fine features. See M01 §7.

4 · Threshold voltage

VT = φMS − QiCox − QdCox + 2φF
(3.1)
VT threshold voltage (V) · φMS gate-to-semiconductor work function difference (V) · Qi total interface and oxide charge per unit area (C/cm²) · Qd depletion charge per unit area (C/cm²), signed · Cox oxide capacitance per unit area (F/cm²) · φF Fermi potential (V), signed. The professor's four-term form. Signs per the table in §2.1. The first two terms dominate. Taught in M03.
Cox = εrε0tox
(3.4)
Cox capacitance per unit area (F/cm²) · εr relative permittivity, 3.9 for SiO₂ (dimensionless) · ε0 = 8.85×10⁻¹⁴ F/cm · tox oxide thickness (cm). Area is already divided out. Reference value: tox = 1000 Å = 10⁻⁵ cm gives Cox = 3.45×10⁻⁸ F/cm².
Qi = qNi
(3.5)
Qi charge per unit area (C/cm²) · q = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C · Ni areal density of charges (cm⁻²), 5×10¹⁰ to 10¹³ cm⁻² depending on process quality. The conversion step everyone forgets. Sources quote the density; the equation needs the charge.
φF = ± kTq ln (Nni)
(3.8)
φF Fermi potential (V) · kT/q = 0.0259 V at 300 K · N body doping, NA or ND (cm⁻³) · ni = 1.5×10¹⁰ cm⁻³ (course convention). Plus for a p-type body, minus for an n-type body — the minus must be written explicitly, because substituting ND returns a positive number. Natural logarithm. Reference value: 0.288 V at 10¹⁵ cm⁻³.
Wm = √4εsφFqN
(3.6)
Wm maximum depletion width (cm) · εs = 11.8 × 8.85×10⁻¹⁴ = 1.044×10⁻¹² F/cm · φF magnitude of the Fermi potential (V) · q = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C · N body doping (cm⁻³). The factor 4 comes from the strong-inversion surface potential being 2φF. Use the magnitude of φF; the sign is carried elsewhere. Answer in cm. Reference value: 0.867×10⁻⁴ cm = 0.867 µm at NA = 10¹⁵ cm⁻³.
Qd = −qNAWm  (p body)  ·   Qd = +qNDWm  (n body)
(3.7)
Qd depletion charge per unit area (C/cm²), signed · NA, ND doping (cm⁻³) · Wm maximum depletion width (cm). A p body exposes ionised acceptors, which are negative; an n body exposes ionised donors, which are positive. Reference value: 1.39×10⁻⁸ C/cm² at 10¹⁵ cm⁻³ with Wm = 0.867 µm.
φMS = φM − φS,   φS = χ + Eg2q ± φF
(3.3)
φM gate work function (V; 4.1 for aluminium) · φS silicon work function (V) · χ electron affinity of silicon, 4.05 V · Eg/2q = 0.56 V · φF Fermi potential magnitude (V). Plus for a p-type body, minus for an n-type body. In this course φMS is normally read from the published graph against doping, not computed; the formula is the consistency check. Graph value for Al on p-Si at 10¹⁵ cm⁻³ is −0.9 V; the formula gives −0.80 V. Silicon's work function spans 4.05 V (Fermi level at Ec) to 5.17 V (Fermi level at Ev), which is why polysilicon gate doping is a design variable.
Nnet = |ND − NA|
(3.9)
Nnet net doping after compensation (cm⁻³) · ND, NA donor and acceptor concentrations (cm⁻³). Subtract the concentrations, not the exponents. 10¹⁶ − 10¹⁵ = 9×10¹⁵, not 10¹. The material is n-type if ND > NA and p-type otherwise. Assumes full ionisation, which holds at room temperature for the shallow dopants used here.

5 · Scaling and the gate dielectric

LEtox ≈ 45
(2.2)
LE gate length · tox oxide thickness, both in the same length unit. Dimensionless. A design rule, not a physical law: it records the ratio at which gate control over the channel stayed adequate against the drain. Points on it: 1 µm → 200 Å, 0.18 µm → 40 Å, < 0.1 µm → 20 Å. M02 §4.
E [eV] = 1.24λ [µm]
(3.2)
E photon energy (eV) · λ free-space wavelength (µm). The constant is hc/q; its exact value is 1.2398 eV·µm. Works in both directions. Silicon (Eg = 1.12 eV) absorbs below 1.107 µm; GaN (3.4 eV) below 0.365 µm. Also sets lithography exposure wavelengths.
EOT = thigh-k · 3.9εr,high-k
(7.1)
EOT equivalent oxide thickness (nm or Å) · thigh-k physical thickness of the alternative dielectric · εr,high-k its relative permittivity (dimensionless). The thickness of SiO₂ that would give the same capacitance per unit area. The entire point of a high-k dielectric: tunnelling depends on the physical thickness while the transistor responds to the equivalent one, so a thicker layer behaves electrically like a thin one and leaks far less. Full treatment in M07, not yet built.

6 · Oxide charge and breakdown

Qi = Qf + Qit + Qot + Qm
(5.3)
Qf fixed oxide charge, positive, within ~2 nm of the interface, set by the oxidation and the crystal orientation (C/cm²) · Qit interface trapped charge, at the interface, from dangling bonds; also quoted as Dit in states/cm²·eV · Qot oxide trapped charge, in the bulk, positive or negative, anneals out above 550 °C · Qm mobile ionic charge, Na⁺/K⁺/Li⁺, drifts under bias. All areal densities in C/cm². Qf and Qm are well controlled today; Qit and Qot are the reliability problem because they are generated during operation. M05 §6.
ΔVT = − QoxCox
(5.4)
ΔVT threshold shift (V) · Qox effective oxide charge per unit area (C/cm²) · Cox oxide capacitance per unit area (F/cm²). Positive oxide charge gives a negative shift. Charge sitting a distance x from the gate in an oxide of thickness tox counts only x/tox of its value, so the bare sum of (5.3) is exact for interface charge and an upper bound for charge distributed through the bulk. Reference value: an interface trap density of 6.8×10¹¹ cm⁻² through a 20 nm oxide gives 0.63 V.
Eox = Vtox
(5.2)
Eox oxide field (V/cm) · V voltage across the oxide (V) · tox oxide thickness (cm). Uniform-field approximation for a parallel-plate structure. Convert thickness to cm: 5 nm = 5×10⁻⁷ cm. Thresholds to compare against: 8–10 MV/cm ramped breakdown for a high-quality dry oxide, the lower end being the minimum a film must demonstrate to be accepted; ~5 MV/cm typical CMOS operating field. Time to breakdown falls by roughly a decade per 0.3–0.5 MV/cm, which is why the operating field sits so far below the breakdown field.
Nit = f · Nsurf
(5.5)
Nit areal density of interface traps (cm⁻²) · f fraction of surface bonds left dangling (dimensionless) · Nsurf areal density of silicon atoms on the surface plane, 6.8×10¹⁴ cm⁻² for (100). Assumes one electronic charge per state. The worked case: f = 10⁻³ gives 6.8×10¹¹ cm⁻², which through a 20 nm oxide shifts the threshold by 0.63 V. A 99.9% perfect interface is not good enough, which is the measure of how good the Si/SiO₂ system has to be.
JFN = q2Eox216π2ℏφox exp (− 4(2qm*)1/2φox3/23ℏEox )
(6.1)
JFN Fowler–Nordheim tunnelling current density (A/cm²) · q 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C · Eox oxide field (V/cm) · ℏ 1.055×10⁻³⁴ J·s · φox Si–SiO₂ barrier in volts, 3.1 V (so qφox = 3.1 eV) · m* electron effective mass in the oxide (kg). Valid only while the tunnelling distance φox/Eox is shorter than tox; otherwise the transport is direct tunnelling and has no closed form. The identification test: log(J/Eox2) against 1/Eox is a straight line for F–N and is not for anything else.
Jgate ≤ Ioff/WL = 1 A/cm2
(6.2)
Jgate gate tunnelling current density (A/cm²) · Ioff/W off-state subthreshold leakage per unit channel width, taken as 10 nA/µm · L gate length (µm). The criterion is that gate leakage must not exceed the source–drain leakage already present. Worked: 10 nA/µm over a 1 µm × 1 µm channel is 10⁻⁸ A over 1 µm² = 10⁻⁸ A/µm²; since 1 cm² = 10⁸ µm², that is 1 A/cm². Watch this conversion — it is the one place in this course where µm² and cm² must be converted, and the factor is 10⁸, not 10⁴.
1Ctot = 1Cpoly dep + 1Cox
(6.3)
Ctot total gate capacitance per unit area (F/cm²) · Cpoly dep polysilicon depletion capacitance per unit area (F/cm²) · Cox oxide capacitance per unit area (F/cm²), see §4. Series capacitors, so the smaller one dominates. Cox grows as the oxide thins while Cpoly dep does not, so poly depletion starts to bite below tox ≈ 10 nm. It raises the equivalent oxide thickness, lowers inversion charge, lowers ID. Heavier poly doping narrows the depleted layer and reduces the effect.
tox = a(tg + τ)b
(6.4)
tox oxide thickness (Å) · tg growth time · τ the time that would have been needed to grow the oxide already present on the surface, which shifts the time origin · a, b fitting constants. Reisman & Nicollian (1987), empirical, dry O₂. Use it where Deal–Grove does not apply: Deal–Grove predicts films above 350 Å well and fails below 200 Å for dry oxidation — the anomalous region. Equation (6.4) is a fit, not a mechanism.
dtoxdt = B2tox + A + C exp (− toxL2 )
(6.5)
tox oxide thickness · t time · A, B the ordinary Deal–Grove linear and parabolic rate constants · C = C0exp(−EA/kT), with C0 ≈ 3.6×10⁸ µm/hr and EA ≈ 2.35 eV · L2 ≈ 7 nm. Massoud’s thin-oxide correction: Deal–Grove plus a term that decays over L2. Dry oxidation, lightly doped substrates, 800–1000 °C, independent of orientation. This is the model in the process simulators, not (6.4) — it preserves A and B, so all the other oxidation physics expressed in them carries over.

7 · Wafers and crystal growth

A2A1 = (D2D1)2
(4.4)
A wafer area · D wafer diameter, both in any consistent unit; the ratio is dimensionless. 200 → 300 mm gives 2.25. Usable die count rises slightly more, because the fixed edge-exclusion zone is a smaller fraction of a larger wafer.
vrate ∝ 1Dcrystal
(4.3)
vrate Czochralski pull rate (mm/min) · Dcrystal crystal diameter (mm). A slower pull gives a larger diameter, because the latent heat released at the freezing front must be conducted away through the growing crystal. A larger wafer therefore costs growth time directly: a 300 mm boule takes about 100 hours.
SiO₂ + 2C → Si + 2CO
Si + 3HCl ⇌ SiHCl₃ + H₂
(4.1–4.2)
Carbothermic reduction of silica to metallurgical-grade silicon (98% pure), then the reversible trichlorosilane reaction used forward to make a distillable liquid and backward, in the Siemens reactor, to deposit electronic-grade polysilicon (99.9999%). Class temperatures: about 1400 °C and about 900 °C respectively; see M04 §2 for the published values, which differ substantially.
Si + 2NaOH + H₂O → Na₂SiO₃ + 2H₂
Si + 4NaOH → Na₄SiO₄ + 2H₂
(4.5)
The two chemical reactions of silicon CMP: sodium metasilicate and sodium orthosilicate. The mechanism is chemical attack followed by mechanical abrasion of the soft reaction product, which is why CMP removes material smoothly rather than scratching. Roughness falls from about 2.5 µm before polishing to under 100 Å after.

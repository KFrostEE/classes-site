The unit conventions behind most wrong answers

Centimetres throughout. The course works in CGS-derived units, not SI. The permittivity of free space is ε0 = 8.85×10⁻¹⁴ F/cm, not 8.85×10⁻¹² F/m. Capacitance is per unit area in F/cm², doping in cm⁻³, areal densities in cm⁻², charge per unit area in C/cm². Convert every length to centimetres before substituting — 1 Å = 10⁻⁸ cm, 1 nm = 10⁻⁷ cm, 1 µm = 10⁻⁴ cm — and depletion widths come out in cm.

The exceptions. The Fowler–Nordheim current (6.1) is SI-native: Eox in V/m, the result in A/m², then 1 A/m² = 10⁻⁴ A/cm². Fed V/cm, its exponent is inflated by exactly 100 and the equation returns zero while the prefactor still looks reasonable. The growth laws carry the units of their fitted constants: Deal–Grove (6.5) and Massoud (6.8) in µm and hours — so Massoud's characteristic length L2 ≈ 7 nm enters as 0.007 µm — Reisman–Nicollian (6.6)–(6.7) in nm and minutes. The gate-leakage ceiling (6.2) is computed in A/µm² and converted with 10⁸ µm²/cm², not 10⁴. The tunnelling probability (7.4) is SI as well, the thickness in m and the barrier in J. The memory-cell capacitance (7b.3) is worked in metres, ε0 = 8.854×10⁻¹² F/m, with capacitance densities in F/m² (80 fF/µm² = 0.080 F/m²), and the Arrhenius step (7b.4) takes k in eV/K and T in kelvin.

Densities are not charges. Interface and oxide charge are quoted as areal number densities in cm⁻², and every equation here needs a charge in C/cm². Multiply by q = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C first. Omitting that step gives an answer wrong by about nineteen orders of magnitude; a unit check catches it, since cm⁻² divided by F/cm² is F⁻¹, not V.

2 · How solutions work in this subject

2.1 Signs in the threshold equation

Two of the four terms change sign with the device type, and the result of a sign error still looks plausible.

Termn-channel (p body)p-channel (n body)Rule
φMS−− Negative for Al and for n⁺ poly on either body type
−Qi/Cox−− Qi is positive charge in both cases
−Qd/Cox+− Qd < 0 for a p body (ionised acceptors), > 0 for an n body (ionised donors)
+2φF+− φF > 0 for p type, < 0 for n type

Consequence to check against. For an n-channel device the last two terms share a sign and partially offset the first two; in the M03 §7 device they come to +0.98 V together. With an aluminium or n⁺-polysilicon gate a p-channel device has all four terms negative, so its threshold is large and negative, about twice the magnitude of an n-channel device on the same wafer with the same oxide (−4.919 V against −2.24 V in the course pair); with such a gate, a small or positive p-channel threshold has a sign wrong. A p⁺-polysilicon gate makes φMS positive and can legitimately give a small or even positive p-channel threshold (M02 §8). Lecture 3 draws from this asymmetry that the p-channel device set the supply: about six volts in the 1970s and early 1980s in the lecture's account, while the published record gives about −24 to −28 V for the metal-gate p-channel logic of the mid-to-late 1960s (M03 §6).

2.2 Order-of-magnitude checks

  • Cox is 3.45×10⁻⁸ F/cm² at 1000 Å and scales inversely with thickness: 1.73×10⁻⁷ at 200 Å, 3.45×10⁻⁷ at 100 Å, 6.90×10⁻⁷ at 50 Å, 1.73×10⁻⁶ at 20 Å, 2.30×10⁻⁶ F/cm² at 15 Å.
  • φF is 0.288 V at 10¹⁵ cm⁻³ and rises by 0.0259 × ln 10 = 0.060 V per decade of doping: 0.347 V at 10¹⁶, 0.407 V at 10¹⁷.
  • Wm is 0.867 µm at 10¹⁵ cm⁻³ and falls roughly as the inverse square root of the doping, about 3.2× per decade, less the slow growth of φF. A junction against a 10²⁰ cm⁻³ side at 5 V reverse bias depletes 2.77, 0.880 and 0.280 µm into bodies of 10¹⁵, 10¹⁶ and 10¹⁷ cm⁻³.
  • An interface charge density of 5×10¹¹ cm⁻² through a 1000 Å oxide costs 2.32 V; the shift is linear in density and in thickness. One dangling bond in 1000 on (100) through 20 nm costs 0.63 V.
  • At 10 MV/cm each nanometre of oxide holds 1 V: 5 V across 5 nm, 2 V across 2 nm. A spot 30% thinner than its surroundings carries 1/0.7 ≈ 1.4 times the field.
  • An oxidation consumes 0.46 of its own thickness in silicon: 1 µm of oxide takes 0.46 µm.
  • The Fowler–Nordheim tunnelling distance at 8 MV/cm is 3.1 V ÷ 8×10⁶ V/cm = 3.9 nm, and the measured F–N current there, about 5×10⁻⁷ A/cm², is six decades below the 1 A/cm² leakage ceiling.
  • The ceiling is 1 A/cm² at 10 nA/µm and a 1 µm gate, and scales as (Ioff/W)/L.
  • Polysilicon depletion at 10²⁰ cm⁻³ and 5 MV/cm adds 3.6 Å of oxide-equivalent thickness: 3.5% of the gate capacitance at 100 Å, 19% at 15 Å.
  • A high-k film is physically thicker than its EOT by k/3.9: about six times for HfO₂ at k = 24, 2.3 times for Al₂O₃. In a stack the SiO₂ interlayer counts in full, so no k takes the EOT below it.
  • The ideal subthreshold swing is 59.5 mV/decade at 300 K. At a 0.3 V threshold, 80 against 60 mV/decade is 1.25 decades, about 18 times, more off current.
  • One femtofarad at one volt holds 1 fC, 6.24×10³ electrons; 30 fF at 1 V holds 1.9×10⁵.
  • ALD adds about an ångström per cycle: 0.83 Å from the notes' 200 Å/min at 4 cycles/s, 1.1–1.2 Å measured for TMA with water.
  • A reaction-limited CVD rate with Ea = 1.3 eV doubles every 25 °C near 467 °C.

11 · High-k gate dielectrics

The capacitance per unit area of any film, C = kε0/t, is (7.1), listed with (3.4) in §4. The notes write the dielectric constant k, ε, εox and εhigh-k for the same dimensionless ratio; this section writes k, and ε0 only for the permittivity of free space. Material values are in §15.11.

k = n2 + klat,    klat ∝ N Z*2μ ωTO2
(7.2)
k static dielectric constant (dimensionless) · n optical refractive index (dimensionless); n² is the electronic part · klat the ionic, or lattice, part (dimensionless) · N number of ions per unit volume · Z* transverse, or Born, effective charge, in units of the electron charge · μ reduced mass of the cation–anion pair · ωTO angular frequency of the transverse optical phonon. A proportionality, written to show the dependences, so it fixes no unit system; Robertson (2004, eq. 11) gives the full expression. With ωTO2 = f/μ, f the restoring force constant (N/m), the denominator is f and the ionic masses cancel. Applies well below the infrared, which includes every frequency a transistor operates at. Beyond the notes. SiO₂: n ≈ 1.46, so about 2.1 of its 3.9 is electronic and 1.8 ionic; HfO₂: roughly 4 electronic and 20 ionic of about 24. A large k needs a large Z* (5.85 for Hf in cubic HfO₂, against a nominal +4) and a soft polar vibration. The electronic structure that gives a large k gives a small gap — in HfO₂, ZrO₂ and Ta₂O₅ the conduction band is formed from the metal's empty d orbitals — so k and Eg run inversely. Taught in M07 §2.

Ta₂O₅ from TAETO (Wolf): reaction-limited between 450 and 485 °C with Ea = 1.3 eV, mass-flow limited above 485 °C. At 467 °C (740 K): 8.617×10⁻⁵ × 740² × 0.693 ÷ 1.3 = 25 K, so the rate doubles every 25 °C. The reaction-limited regime is the conformal one, which is why the DRAM film is deposited there. Taught in M07b §5.1.
Ti% = [Ti][Ba] + [Sr] + [Ti] × 100
(7b.5)

Oxygen is not counted. A stoichiometric perovskite, one A cation (Ba or Sr) per Ti, gives exactly 50%. The notes (p. 63): the highest capacitance density is at a slight excess, about 51%, and the titanium incorporated must be tightly controlled. Wolf: k drops by about 40% from 51% to 53%. The conversions that are not multiplications. A number density in cm⁻² becomes a charge density in C/cm² by multiplying by q, and that is the step most often skipped. An energy in eV is used directly as a potential in V: dividing by the electronic charge gives the same number. A per-unit-area capacitance in F/cm² becomes the capacitance of a real gate by multiplying by the gate area in cm², and gate areas start life in µm², so convert them with the 10⁻⁸ that squaring 10⁻⁴ produces. A temperature interval is the same in kelvin and in degrees Celsius, so the ΔT×2 of (7b.4) reads either way, but T itself must be absolute. A memory's size is counted in bits: the notes' "256 MB" and "4 GB" are the 256 Mb and 4 Gb generations, not a factor of eight larger.

The subject in one picture

An integrated circuit is a device that controls small quantities of charge inside small volumes, and it does so reliably only because the conditions it sits in are narrow and stable. Space supplies none of those conditions. It removes the air that carries heat away, widens the temperature range by hundreds of degrees, fills the volume with energetic particles that deposit charge and displace atoms, shakes the hardware violently on the way up, limits the available power to what a solar array can generate, and makes repair impossible.

The course is the study of what each of those conditions does to a device, and what is done about it.

The shape of the course

An environment — radiation, temperature, vacuum, mechanical load — acts on a material, changing its charge population or its lattice. That change moves a device parameter: a threshold voltage, a leakage current, a carrier lifetime, a bond strength. The parameter shift degrades or destroys a system. Design, testing and simulation exist to predict where along that chain a given mission will fail.

The order of the modules follows that chain. Part I establishes what the electronics is and why space is hard for it — the device physics the rest of the course runs on, the commercial technology it runs behind, and the requirements and economics that keep it there. Part II builds the environment: what radiation is, where the Sun's output comes from, how Earth's magnetic field traps particles, and what a given orbit consequently sees. Part III takes each mechanism by which radiation couples to matter, and Part IV covers SRIM, the simulation tool the course is built around. Part V applies all of it to devices, and Part VI covers the rest of the environment — temperature, packaging, reliability, power and detectors.

Modules

Part I · The electronics, and why space is hard for it

M01 · ~75 min
Semiconductors and Devices

Bands from atoms and from periodicity, carriers and the Fermi level, dopant levels and the temperature regimes, band bending, the p–n junction, the MOS capacitor's four regimes, and the MOSFET as a barrier-controlled device.
M02 · ~45 min
Scaling of Electronics

Moore's law and the power wall, planar to fin to nanosheet, why the node name measures nothing, standard-cell scaling, and lithography from i-line to EUV.
M03 · ~35 min
Space Electronics and the Technology Gap

What qualification demands, heat rejection with no air, why the volume economics fail, technology readiness and flight heritage, and the RAD750 as the case.

Part II · The space environment

M04 · ~45 min
Radiation: Species, Decay, and Laboratory Sources

Radiation with mass against radiation without; alpha, beta, proton, neutron, heavy ion, X-ray and gamma; the penetration ordering and why charge explains it; decay, half-life and activity; why Cs-137 and Co-60 are the test standards.
M05 · ~40 min
The Sun and the Particle Populations of Deep Space

The proton–proton chain and what it emits, the solar photon and particle spectra, solar wind, flares and coronal mass ejections, the eleven-year cycle, and galactic cosmic rays.
M06 · ~45 min
Earth's Magnetosphere and the Trapped Belts

The geomagnetic field, the Lorentz force, and the three trapped motions. Why the inner belt holds protons and the outer belt electrons, and how the belts move with solar activity.
M07 · ~40 min
Orbits and the Environment They See

LEO, MEO, GEO, HEO, the Lagrange points and interplanetary space; the radiation-risk table at solar minimum and maximum; and why GPS accepts the worst orbit of all.

Part III · How radiation couples to matter

M08 · ~40 min
Ionization and Excitation

The Coulomb interaction at atomic range, why an uncharged particle must score a direct hit, carriers generated per incident particle, delta rays, and what the generated charge does to a junction.
M09 · ~35 min
Bremsstrahlung and Characteristic X-rays

Why a deflected charge radiates, the mass and atomic-number dependences, the X-ray tube spectrum, and filtration and beam hardening.
M10 · ~45 min
Photon Interaction with Matter

Photoelectric absorption, Compton scattering and pair production; which dominates at which energy and atomic number; the exponential attenuation law and the NIST coefficients; and why lead shields and silicon does not.
M11 · ~40 min
Displacement Damage

Knock-on atoms, vacancies and cascades; why a particle that stops inside a die is worse than one that passes through; and the four device consequences of a defect level in the gap.
M12 · ~40 min
Energy Loss, Range, and the Bragg Peak

Stopping power and its electronic and nuclear parts, linear energy transfer and the charge it liberates, range, straggling and the Bragg peak. The quantitative layer that SRIM computes.

Part IV · Simulation

M13 · ~45 min
SRIM and TRIM

What a binary-collision Monte Carlo calculation is and why the problem needs one; setting up an ion and a layered target, compounds and gases; reading range, ionization, phonon, damage and sputtering output; and what the tool does not model.

Part V · Radiation effects on electronics

M14 · ~45 min
Total Ionizing Dose

The three classes of radiation effect; how absorbed energy becomes trapped oxide charge and interface traps; threshold shift, oxide-thickness scaling, annealing and field-oxide leakage; and how a part is tested to a dose.
M15 · pending
Particle Radiation on Electronics: Single-Event Effects

Charge collection and the current transient, critical charge, the soft and hard event taxonomy, cross-section curves, and rate prediction.
M16 · pending
Hardening and Shielding

Shielding mass and the range argument against over-shielding, secondary production, hardening by process, layout, circuit and system, and wide-bandgap materials.

Part VI · The rest of the space environment

M17 · pending
Temperature Extremes

Carrier freeze-out and mobility at cryogenic temperature, leakage and threshold at high temperature, where silicon runs out, and what replaces it for Venus and for the Sun.
M18 · pending
Chip Bonding and Packaging

Wire bond against flip chip, thermal-expansion mismatch and cycling, hermeticity, outgassing and tin whiskers, and why packaging now drives performance.
M19 · pending
Reliability, Failure Analysis, and Qualification

Failure-rate models and the mechanisms behind them, launch vibration and acoustic qualification, thermal vacuum, screening levels, and failure analysis methods.
M20 · pending
Space Power: Generation and Storage

Solar cells and their degradation under radiation, the inverse-square limit on their use, radioisotope thermoelectric generators, and battery temperature limits.
M21 · pending
Detectors and Space Telescopes

Photodetection, CCD against CMOS imagers, infrared detector materials, radiation effects on imagers, and Hubble against the James Webb Space Telescope.
M22 · pending
Emerging Technologies

Wide- and ultra-wide-bandgap devices, micro-electromechanical systems, two-dimensional materials, and radiation energy harvesting.
